Method, limitations and sources

Aruba: median asking price in US dollars × 1.79 ÷ (Afl. 2,912 × 12 × assumed earners). CBS table 7.4 reports a total-economy median monthly wage of Afl. 2,912 at December 31, 2024, the latest year located in this table. Multiplying by 12 annualises that observation; it is not a measured annual income. CBS credits the Social Security Bank and the Government’s Department of Human Resources. Coverage is administrative wages, not all household income.

Prices: Mercala’s January–July median is US$599,500, based on 216 newly observed house listings. July alone is US$799,000 (41 listings); January–June is US$564,000 (182 listings). Period samples differ and must not be added together. Mercala covers active brokerages and listings for sale priced at US$50,000 or above. These are asking prices, not completed sales, and changes can reflect the mix of houses listed. This is not a census of Aruba’s housing stock. July is the latest island-wide monthly article found as of September 30, 2026; the homepage has newer district summaries.

Timing and income: 2024 wages are held fixed against 2026 prices. No wage growth, inflation adjustment, tax adjustment or household-income conversion is assumed. Two median wages are an illustrative scenario, not the median of household incomes. These ratios omit living costs, interest, down payments, taxes and transaction fees.

OECD: Brick by Brick (2021), figure 4.1, based on Bricongne, Turrini and Pontuch (2019) and OECD calculations. We use the workbook’s 2020 P2I rows, retain their observation quarters, and exclude missing observations. The 31 available OECD countries are not the full OECD membership. No extrapolation to 2026 and no Aruba rank or OECD average is calculated.
