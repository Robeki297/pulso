Method, limitations and sources

Aruba’s 100 m² estimate: Properstar publishes a median house asking price of Afl. 5,692/m² for Aruba, updated September 2026. Multiplying by 100 gives Afl. 569,200. We use its houses category, excluding the separate apartment statistic. This is a standardised-size estimate using listings across house sizes, not a filter for exactly 100 m² listings. Price per m² is not constant across house sizes, locations or quality. Properstar does not disclose the sample count or a full floor-area definition on this page; it is a portal sample, not the whole market or completed sales. The page’s summary provides the figure while its time-series charts report no data.

Income: CBS table 7.4 reports a total-economy median monthly wage of Afl. 2,912 at December 31, 2024, the latest year located in that table. Multiplying by 12 annualises that observation to Afl. 34,944; it is not measured annual income. CBS credits the Social Security Bank and the Government’s Department of Human Resources. This is an administrative wage measure, not all household income. Two median wages are a scenario, not the median of household incomes.

Timing: 2024 wages are held fixed against September 2026 prices. No wage growth, inflation adjustment or tax adjustment is assumed. The calculation omits interest, down payments, living costs and transaction fees. The earlier Mercala whole-house median is not used because its public articles do not provide the island-wide floor-area statistic needed for this size adjustment.

OECD: Housing Policy Toolkit, “The housing market”, first chart and its downloadable workbook. We use the unrounded “2022 or latest available” values only where the separate year field is 2022. There are 29 eligible countries; Australia and Estonia have 2021 values and are omitted. The source builds on Bricongne, Turrini and Pontuch’s HouseLev research and the OECD Analytical Database. Its income concept is per-capita gross disposable household income. “Gross” here means before deduction of fixed-capital consumption, not gross wages before personal income tax. The measure is not whole-household income. No ratios are extrapolated or inferred from the standard OECD price-to-income index.
